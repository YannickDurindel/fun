# 11. Niche audience: alpine/mountain pages (Instagram) and Stoic pages (X)

Researched 2026-11-16. Grades: **P** primary (platform, regulator, club, survey owner, raw API data we pulled ourselves), **S** secondary (journalism, blogs, aggregators quoting primary), **U** unverified/estimate. Dates are the data date, not the fetch date, unless stated. Numbered sources are at the end; "[n]" links a claim to a source. Everything not marked as fetched in the source list was not fetched.

## 0. Summary of what the evidence supports

1. The mountain audience is large, growing and demographically broad. The German Alpine Club (DAV) hit 1,638,652 members at 31 Dec 2025 (+4.3%) and the Austrian club (OeAV) 748,046 [P/S, 2026]. Hiking, not climbing, is the dominant activity (95% of surveyed DAV members hike) [P, 2025].
2. Interest in "Stoicism" as an encyclopedic topic is falling, not rising. English Wikipedia views for "Stoicism" are down roughly 45% from the 2022 average to the last-12-month average (our own API pull). The online Stoic scene is real but male-skewed and heavily criticised as "broicism" [P/S, 2024-2026]. This does not prove falling interest in Stoic content on social platforms.
3. Instagram skews female and young in the US (55% women vs 44% men; 80% of 18-29s use it) while X skews male (men more likely to use it; exact % not in the text we retrieved) [P, 2025]. Our two pages therefore sit on platforms with opposite gender tilt, which matches the likely audiences (alpine mood: mixed; Stoic: male-leaning).
4. Seasonality is measurable and strong in German-language avalanche interest (Jan/Feb about 3x Jul/Aug), moderate for "Alps" (Dec/Jan high plus a summer bump), and weak for "Stoicism" (January about 20% above average; no clear summer/winter pattern) [P, our pull of Wikimedia data, 2022-2025].
5. Strongest underserved angles that we could source: avalanche-safety content tied to actual accident statistics, overtourism/"don't geotag" ethics, hut culture (the DAV member survey says huts and trails are the most valued service, 79%), and Stoic practice versus quote spam.

### 1.3 Who follows Stoic accounts

There is no representative survey of Stoic-content followers. What exists:

- r/Stoicism grew from about 840 members (2012) to about 610,000 (2024); #stoicism on TikTok has about 645,000 posts; Ryan Holiday's Daily Stoic is credited with over 10 million copies and 3m+ Instagram followers (Xataka, 25 May 2026, which cites other secondary sources we could not verify) [10] (S/U).
- Maloney, Jones & Roberts (2024), "'I Can Choose to be a Good Man Even if I Got a Raw Deal': Neoliberal Heteromasculinity as Manosphere Counter Narrative in r/Stoicism", *Social Media + Society* 10(3), Aug 2024. Abstract: r/Stoicism is a large subreddit where boys and men seek Stoic advice on varied life matters; mixed computational and qualitative analysis; positioned as a counter-narrative to manosphere discourse [11] (P for existence and abstract; full text and DOI not fetched, 403).
- Ağaoğlu (2025), "Reconfiguring Stoicism: Convergence of Self-Improvement and Masculinity on TikTok", *New Review of Hypermedia and Multimedia*, DOI 10.1080/13614568.2025.2595322. Multimodal discourse analysis of 20 videos with 300,000+ views under #stoic/#stoicism; themes of self-improvement, a "dangerous" persona and traditional masculinity [12] (S: abstract via university repository, closed-access paper not fetched).
- Third-party creator-analytics pages call the core audience of some Stoic YouTube channels young men 18-34, but label this an estimate from public metadata [13] (U).

Reading: the dominant Stoic-content audience is young-adult and male-leaning (U for exact split), and the discourse around it is critical ("broicism", coined by Pigliucci in 2019 per Xataka [10]). A page that looks like quote spam sits in the most criticised, most saturated part of the niche.

## 2. Demand and trends (data we pulled ourselves)

Google Trends could not be fetched (no public API, search tools returned no chart data). Rankhero-style pages claim a flat trend with a Jan 2026 peak but the page's date range ran past the date of the claim and is unreliable [U]. As a transparent substitute, we pulled **monthly Wikipedia page views (all-access, user agents)** from the Wikimedia REST API on 2026-11-16 [14] (P for the raw counts). Wikipedia views measure encyclopedic curiosity, are depressed by AI answer boxes since 2025, and are a weak proxy for social interest. Treat all trend statements below as directional.

| Article | 2022 monthly avg | Last-12-month avg (Nov 2025-Oct 2026) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| en "Stoicism" | about 141,700 | about 77,400 | about -45% |
| it "Stoicismo" | about 10,600 | about 5,500 | about -49% |
| de "Stoizismus" | about 248 | about 169 | about -32% (very small base) |
| en "Alps" | about 52,100 | about 40,200 | about -23% |
| de "Alpen" | about 22,200 | about 17,800 | about -20% |
| fr "Alpes" | about 10,600 | about 7,700 | about -27% |
| it "Alpi" | about 9,700 | about 5,700 | about -41% |
| de "Lawine" | about 2,950 | about 2,290 | about -22% |
| en "Skiing" | about 21,700 | about 11,400 | about -48% |

The broad decline across unrelated topics (including Alps and skiing) suggests a general Wikipedia-traffic decline, not a Stoic-specific collapse. Relative to that background, Stoicism is not outperforming. It does not support the claim that Stoic interest is booming; it also does not show collapse in social usage. Mark: S/U for interpretation.

Event spikes visible in the raw data: en "Marcus Aurelius" jumped to 512,057 (Nov 2024) and 476,014 (Dec 2024) from about 180,000-210,000 in the months before, then decayed back to about 145,000-175,000 in 2026. The timing coincides with a major film release featuring the emperor; that cause is our inference, not sourced [U]. Lesson: Stoic interest is event-driven by pop culture.

en "Avalanche" spiked to 35,946 views in August 2026 against about 10,000-13,000 in neighbouring months; cause not identified [U]. We did not check this against a news event.

Other trend inputs:
- Pinterest Predicts 2026 (21 trends): coverage mentions "Peak Travel" with mountain ranges and outdoor escapes, plus comfort/calm and micro-escapes; no Stoicism trend found in the coverage we saw [15] (S).
- Hashtags: secondary blogs say Mosseri stated hashtags do not improve reach and that keywords in captions/profile/alt text matter more; one blog dates a five-hashtag cap to December 2025; no primary Instagram post was found. One blog cites a 2026 Metricool study (hashtag users averaged 31.7% fewer views; correlation only) [16] (S/U). Public per-hashtag post counts for #alps, #stoic etc. were not obtained, so we cannot quantify saturation by hashtag.

## 3. Seasonality

Averages by calendar month, 2022-2025, from the Wikimedia pull above [14] (P raw data, our computation):

| Topic | Peak months | Trough months | Peak/trough |
|---|---|---|---|
| de "Lawine" | Jan 4,714; Feb 4,602; Dec 3,726 | Jul 1,490; Aug 1,495 | about 3.2x |
| en "Skiing" | Jan 35,456; Feb 33,084 | Jun-Aug about 15,000-15,400 | about 2.3x |
| en "Alps" | Jan 63,195; Dec 59,285; Jul-Aug about 55,000 | Apr 47,903; Nov 49,168 | about 1.3x (double hump: winter and summer) |
| de "Alpen" | Jan 26,243; Aug 25,222; Sep 24,053 | Nov 19,172; Apr 19,644 | about 1.4x (summer-leaning) |
| fr "Alpes" | Jan 15,221; Aug 11,969 | Jun 7,984 | about 1.9x |
| en "Stoicism" | Jan 169,722 | Jun 129,078 | about 1.3x |
| it "Stoicismo" | Jan 14,027; May 13,802 | Jul-Aug about 8,400-8,600 | about 1.7x (Italian summer dip) |

Interpretation:
- Stoicism: a January bump (new-year self-improvement) is visible but modest (about 20% above the yearly mean in English). Whether it is a "habit surge" is our inference [U]. Italian interest drops in July-August.
- Alpine: planning/ski season Dec-Feb and summer alpinism Jul-Sep, with spring (Apr-May) and late autumn (Nov) as troughs. Content built for troughs competes with less demand but also less attention; evergreen mood content is the sensible filler.
- Avalanche safety demand is strongly winter-weighted, so it must be published Nov-Feb and re-surfaced on avalanche-news peaks.

Real-world risk timing supports this: Austria had 26 avalanche deaths by 25 Feb 2026, against a ten-year mean of 16 per full winter (11 by 24 Feb); 15 of the 26 were off-piste ("Variante"), 22 male, 14 non-Austrian; 14 died in 15-24 Feb alone (OeKAS press release, 25 Feb 2026) [17] (P). Switzerland (SLF, to end March 2026): 244 people caught in 171 avalanches vs 10-year means of 182 and 127; 15 deaths in 13 fatal accidents, described as typical; level 5 reached on 17 Feb [18] (S: Le News quoting SLF). Spikes like these are timely hooks.

## 4. Saturation and competition

Evidence is thin; we state only what we found.

- Stoic content: the discourse around TikTok #stoicism is already mass-scale (about 645,000 posts, S/U [10]) and the academic and press literature treats it as formulaic ("dangerous persona", masculinity, grindset) [10][12]. Xataka's thesis (opinion, not data) is that the pop-Stoicism industry does the opposite of what Stoicism teaches [10]. Implication: high saturation in "quote + moody statue" content; positioning on practice or on classical sources is the differentiator (our inference).
- Alpine content: the Dolomites case shows that Instagram/TikTok geotags and an Apple ad (2023, Seceda) drove visitor surges, with Reuters reporting mountain tourism in Italy tripling in a decade and creators like Matteo Perani stopping sharing exact locations (Reuters via The Star, 15 Jan 2026) [19] (S). Scenic-landscape accounts are therefore common, and the backlash against geotag-driven crowding is a live topic. A peer-reviewed Facebook study of a Bulgarian mountaineering group (Cholakova & Dogramadjieva, 2025, *Tourism*) found practical information is searched for and converts into visits [20] (S, abstract only; Facebook, not Instagram).
- Competitor counts, follower distributions and engagement benchmarks for specific alpine/Stoic accounts: not obtained. We are not naming handles because none were verified.

## 6. Language and market choices

- English reaches the largest pool (US about 172-182m Instagram users, India far larger but a different interest mix) but is the most saturated for Stoic content [9] (S).
- German is the home market for Alps content: the DAV (1.64m) and OeAV (0.75m) alone are 2.4m members; Germany has about 31-33m Instagram users [1][3][9]. Italian and French Alps interest exist (it/fr Wikipedia views) but are smaller; the Swiss club and Italian CAI figures were not retrieved.
- Wikipedia German "Stoizismus" is tiny (about 170/month vs 77,400 in English) [14], suggesting German-language Stoic interest is thin, or just that Germans search other terms ("Stoa", "Stoiker"); we did not test synonyms [U].
- Single-language page: X Stoic should be English (audience size and X's Japan/US/UK/India weight, DemandSage undated [8] U). Instagram alpine could be English with German terms as a differentiator; bilingual captions would help keyword matching but cost clarity (our inference).

## 7. Implications for us

Do:
1. Treat the alpine page as a broad, mixed-gender audience (club data 44% women, Pew US Instagram 55% women). Do not design it as a men-only page.
2. Build a winter calendar: avalanche/safety/ski content Nov-Feb with timed posts on avalanche-news peaks; summer alpinism, huts and history Jun-Sep; evergreen mood content in Apr-May and Nov.
3. Use real data in alpine facts (SLF, OeKAS, DAV) with the source and date in the post; this is both a content gap and a trust signal.
4. Make hut culture and mountain ethics (geotagging, crowding) recurring series; both have sourced demand.
5. Position the Stoic page on practice and primary-text context, not quote cards or "dangerous persona" aesthetics, which are the criticised, saturated part [10][12].
6. Use Jan for a Stoic "new year" series, but expect only a modest lift (about 20%, our estimate from Wikipedia views).
7. Prefer a few keywords in captions/profile over many hashtags (secondary evidence only; confirm against the other research units that hold the platform-algorithm primary sources).
8. Pull account-level Insights (country, age, gender) at 1,000 followers and overwrite the estimates in this file.

Do not:
- Do not cite Google Trends numbers from this file; we have none. Pull them manually (trends.google.com) before using them.
- Do not assume Stoic interest is rising; the one measurable proxy fell.
- Do not name or imitate specific accounts based on this file; none were verified.
- Do not publish exact hiking-trail or lake locations as geotags by default.
